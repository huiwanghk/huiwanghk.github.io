About

Hui is a third-year PhD candidate at the Department of Geography, The University of Hong Kong. Under the expert guidance of Professor Becky P.Y. Loo and with the invaluable support of The Joint Lab on Future Cities, he is exploring sustainable transport solutions to ensure the sustainability of future cities. Hui received his Master's degree in Transport Engineering from the College of Transportation Engineering, at Tongji University in June 2022 and the B.E. degree in Railway Transport Engineering from the School of Traffic and Transportation, at Beijing Jiaotong University in June 2019, respectively. He has been awarded several honors, including the China National Scholarship, Outstanding Graduate Award of Shanghai, and Outstanding Master's Thesis award.

His previous training encompasses both engineering and social sciences and his current research focuses on developing people-oriented and place-based policies to facilitate safe, inclusive, efficient, and healthy transport systems, contributing transformative changes to next-generation mobility.

Research Interests:
※ Transport planning, travel behavior, and urban form
※ Rail operations and agent-based simulation
※ Walkability, health, and spatial analysis

Projects

Public Transport Disadvantage

[Video] The public transport disadvantaged in a highly transit-oriented city: An analytical framework, key challenges and opportunities.

Accomplishments
  • A holistic framework to identify public transport disadvantaged areas.
  • Public transport accessibility to a broad range of public facilities is considered.
  • Walking to transit stops, not waiting for vehicles, is a rural transport problem.
  • Rural areas struggle to access education, healthcare, & shopping through public transport.
  • Rural areas need 90+ min for similar opportunities as urban areas within 30 min.
Dynamics of in-station time

[Video] Dynamics of in-station time within metro systems: Measurement and determining factors.

Accomplishments
  • An agent-based simulation to measure the dynamics of in-station time.
  • Considered different relevant factors and their effects on in-station time.
  • In-station time can be substantial (>25 min).
  • Navigation applications should consider and model in-station time explicitly.
  • Responsive solutions include prioritizing inbound and transfer passenger flows.
Demand Responsive Transit

Adaptability evaluation method of Route Deviation Demand Responsive Transit.

Accomplishments
  • Summarizes the research methods of domestic and foreign DRT adaptability.
  • A techno-economic assessment method is used to support the analysis of the RdDRT adaptability.
  • A simulation modeling of RdDRT is created to support the technical and economic evaluation model.
  • Selecting Shanghai Jiading No. 66 bus as the research case.
